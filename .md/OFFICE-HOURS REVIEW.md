## Competitive Landscape

| Product | Transparency | Replay | Prop firm controls | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Generic MT5 EA (most ICT bots) | None — black box | No | Manual SL only | $50-200 one-time |
| Signal-following Telegram bots | None | No | None | Free / $10-30/mo |
| **Glass Box** | Full ICT checklist per trade | Yes — candle-by-candle | Automatic halt, daily drawdown tracking | $20-49/mo |

No direct competitor combines ICT-specific rule transparency with session replay and prop firm risk controls.

## Problem Statement

Retail traders using ICT (Inner Circle Trader) methodology face three compounding problems:
1. They know the strategy but can't execute it without emotion.
2. When they run automated bots, they have no visibility into why the bot traded — making drawdowns impossible to diagnose.
3. Prop firm challenge candidates get eliminated not because the strategy failed, but because they couldn't monitor whether the bot was following the firm's rules in real time.

Glass Box solves all three: deterministic ICT execution (removes emotion), full transparency dashboard (every decision visible), and built-in prop firm risk controls (automatic halt on drawdown breach).

## Demand Evidence

- Direct conversations with ICT traders who expressed frustration with black-box bots
- The core pain behavior: when a bot draws down, traders can't determine if the strategy broke or if it's normal variance — so they either abandon a working system or keep running a broken one
- Secondary insight surfaced in this session: traders from Telegram signal-following communities ("they don't know what's going on, and they can't blame anyone if it fails") represent a passive-investor segment who want a shared audit trail — not for themselves, but so an experienced trader can review the logs and tell them what happened
- The "hope for the best" status quo is the most expensive behavior in the space: prop firm challenge candidates who fail because they couldn't halt a bot mid-drawdown are paying $500–$1,000+ each time they restart

## Status Quo

The workaround today is: **accept the black box and trust the backtest**. Most ICT bot users have no diagnostic tool when things go wrong. Their options are: (a) manually reconstruct what happened from MT5 trade history, (b) watch the charts live during sessions and annotate by hand, or (c) turn off the bot entirely and go manual when they get nervous. All three are high-cost, low-signal workarounds. None of them give a real answer.

## Target Users & Segmentation

**Tier 1 — $20/mo Starter: The Telegram Signal Follower**
- Who: Passive investor following ICT-adjacent signal channels, primarily from emerging markets where retail Telegram trading communities are large and tight-knit
- Job to be done: Auto-execute signals from a source they trust, and have an audit log they can share with an experienced trader to explain what happened
- What they need: Reliable MT5 execution + shareable trade history + someone else can interpret it for them
- Acquisition: Telegram signal provider partnerships — providers get Glass Box credibility ("audit trail powered by Glass Box"), followers get automated execution
- Word of mouth: High. These communities are relationship-driven

**Tier 2 — $49/mo Pro (V1 — live once billing integration ships): The Prop Firm Challenge Candidate**
- Who: ICT-educated trader currently running or about to run an FTMO/similar challenge, running a bot, and terrified of a single drawdown rule violation ending the challenge
- Job to be done: Know in real time that the bot is following the rules. Be able to audit any trade if challenged.
- What they need: Live Mode (real-time dashboard showing the bot's rule checks as every candle forms and patterns are detected), daily drawdown halt, Prop Firm Panel (tracks progress against firm rules — daily loss remaining, consecutive losses, profit target progress), Replay Mode (scrub back through any past session candle-by-candle to audit exactly why any trade was taken)
- Acquisition: Prop firm challenge communities, YouTube ICT content, comparison against generic MT5 EAs
- Word of mouth: Medium-high. Each success story (challenge passed, payout received) is a strong testimonial

## Constraints

- MT5-only for V1 — the prop firm and ICT communities are predominantly MT5 users; this is acceptable
- No paper trading mode yet — live trading from day one is required; this should be disclosed clearly in onboarding
- Billing integration not yet live — this is the next critical build to unblock any monetization
- Core engine and dashboard are operational; the product can be put in front of users today

## Premises

1. **Two simultaneous target segments from day one.** The $20/mo Starter and $49/mo Pro segments can be acquired in parallel because the core product already serves both. The acquisition motions are different (community/Telegram vs. intent/SEO/content), so splitting marketing focus is manageable.

2. **Glass Box competes on verifiability, not performance.** The ICT automation market competes on win rate and backtest stats. Glass Box competes on a different axis: can you confirm for yourself that the bot is doing what it says? This is orthogonal to performance and creates a positioning moat that is extremely hard for generic EA providers to copy.

3. **MT5-only execution is acceptable for V1.** The prop firm and ICT communities are overwhelmingly MT5. Broadening to cTrader or NinjaTrader is post-revenue work.

4. **$20/mo for the Starter tier is the right entry price.** For the Telegram signal follower segment, this must be a no-brainer impulse decision. $20/mo is below the threshold of deliberation. Volume and word-of-mouth are the Starter tier's value. The Pro tier at $49/mo is priced as insurance, not a tool — at roughly 5% of a single FTMO challenge fee ($1,000), the math is obvious for any serious challenge candidate.

5. **Replay Mode is the single most differentiating feature for the Pro tier.** No ICT automation competitor has session-level, candle-by-candle replay of the bot's decision process. This is what turns Glass Box from "another ICT EA" into "the only product that shows you everything."

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Community-First (Telegram/Discord)
Partner with Telegram signal providers as the primary distribution channel. Signal providers promote Glass Box to their audiences; followers auto-execute with Glass Box attached. The provider gets a credibility boost ("audit-trail powered by Glass Box"); followers get execution + logs they can share.
- Effort: M
- Risk: Low
- Pros: Distribution already exists; signal providers have incentive; word of mouth is extremely high in these communities
- Cons: Dependent on signal provider quality; churn follows the provider's performance, not yours

### Approach B: Prop Firm-First (High-Intent Performance)
Launch Pro at $49/mo alongside Starter. Create content specifically targeting FTMO/prop firm challenge candidates: "The only ICT bot that gives you an audit trail for your challenge." SEO, YouTube, community positioning.
- Effort: M
- Risk: Medium (smaller market, more skeptical audience)
- Pros: High willingness to pay; clear ROI tied to challenge fee; every payout story is a testimonial
- Cons: Prop firm candidates are skeptical of bot claims; longer trust-building cycle

### Approach C: Dual-Launch, Starter-First Bias (Recommended)
Launch both tiers simultaneously with separate messaging. Spend the first 30 days focused on Starter acquisition through Telegram signal provider partnerships. Let word-of-mouth from Starter users pull in Pro-tier interest organically. The two tiers cross-promote: a Starter user who starts a prop firm challenge has a natural upgrade path to Pro.
- Effort: M (two acquisition tracks requiring different skill sets — Telegram relationship-building vs. prop firm content production)
- Risk: Medium
- Pros: Both markets captured from day one; $49 Pro anchors the $20 Starter as affordable; upgrade path is natural; community word-of-mouth from Starter tier is fast
- Cons: Split focus is real — the Telegram relationship-building and the prop firm content are distinct skills

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C — Dual-Launch, Starter-First bias.**

First 30 days: Identify 3–5 Telegram ICT signal providers and offer them a **free Pro account (6-month, no revenue share required)** in exchange for a single promotional post to their channel. The pitch: "Your followers can now auto-execute your signals, and you get an audit trail dashboard that proves your strategy is working. Makes you look more professional." This costs nothing and puts you in front of hundreds to thousands of traders who are already primed to spend.

Simultaneously: Finish the billing integration and the landing page. Without billing, nothing converts. The landing page should have two clear tracks: "I want to auto-execute signals" (Starter) and "I'm running a prop firm challenge" (Pro).

## Open Questions

1. **[Legal — pre-launch blocker]** Does executing trades on behalf of Telegram signal followers constitute unlicensed copy-trading or investment advice in the target markets (Nigeria, UK, etc.)? Must be resolved before a public launch. Beta/waitlist-only until this is cleared. **Fallback if review is unfavorable:** pivot Starter tier positioning to "personal automation tool" (user sets up their own ICT rules, signals not copied from a third party) and remove any language that implies Glass Box executes trades on behalf of a signal provider.
2. What's the churn model if a signal provider stops posting? Mitigation options: (a) make trade history portable so users stay even if they switch providers, (b) allow users to connect multiple signal sources so no single provider controls retention. Note: mark option (b) as post-V1 scope only — it's a non-trivial product feature that should not pull engineering focus now.
3. Pro-tier natural churn: prop firm candidates who pass their challenge stop needing a challenge-protection tool. Retention path: reframe the Pro tier value for funded traders as "audit trail for your funded account" — the need for accountability doesn't go away after the challenge.
3. Onboarding flow for non-technical Starter users who have no MT5 experience — this is a UX problem, filed in the engineering backlog.

## Success Criteria

**30 days:** Billing integration live. Landing page live (two tracks: signal follower + prop firm candidate). At least 1 signal provider partnership active. Legal/compliance review complete or beta-gated launch in place.
**60 days:** 10 paying Starter users. First Pro account sold. Month-1 churn below 20%.
**90 days:** Word-of-mouth referral rate visible in attribution data. At least one prop firm challenge success story documented. Month-2 churn below 15%.

## Distribution Plan

Existing deployment pipeline covers execution (Python on Windows VM + Supabase + Redis). Web dashboard is deployed. The distribution bottleneck is marketing, not infrastructure:
- Starter: Telegram signal provider partnerships → direct DM to providers
- Pro: Landing page SEO + prop firm community posts (FTMO community, The5ers Discord, ICT YouTube comment sections)
- Upgrade path: In-app nudge from Starter to Pro when a user adds a second broker account or mentions a prop firm challenge in the settings

## Dependencies

- **Billing integration** — critical path blocker. Nothing monetizes until this is live. Top engineering priority; estimate and schedule this first.
- **Legal/compliance review** — pre-public-launch blocker for signal follower use case. Run beta/waitlist mode until resolved.
- **Landing page** — in progress. Must have two audience tracks (signal follower vs. prop firm candidate).
- **Settings page** — must ship before the in-app upgrade nudge can be triggered. Sequencing: Settings page → upgrade nudge (in-app nudge depends on Settings page being live).
- **Signal provider outreach** — human effort, can start today with zero code
- **Provider-facing shareable audit view** — the B2B2C motion depends on signal providers being able to see their followers' Glass Box results. At minimum, this means a shareable audit link (public URL for a trade session or history summary) that providers can check without logging into each follower's account. This is a small feature with a big unlock: without it, providers cannot act as the expert interpreters that the Starter tier acquisition model depends on.
